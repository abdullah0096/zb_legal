01 — Family & Matrimonial

Family & Matrimonial

Matrimonial disputes are the matters where the legal answer and the human answer diverge most often. We look for the settlement first, and litigate properly when there is no settlement to be had.

Work spans divorce by mutual consent and contested petitions, restitution of conjugal rights, judicial separation, maintenance under the Hindu Marriage Act and Section 144 BNSS, custody and guardianship, and protection orders under the Domestic Violence Act, 2005.

Where a matter involves children, we will tell you plainly which arguments help and which ones the court will hold against you.

Discuss a family matter

What this covers

  • Divorce — mutual consent and contested
  • Maintenance and interim maintenance
  • Child custody, guardianship and visitation
  • Domestic violence — protection and residence orders
  • Restitution of conjugal rights, judicial separation
  • Mediation and negotiated settlement
  • Muslim personal law matters — talaq, khula, mehr

Bring to the first meeting

  • Marriage certificate or proof of marriage
  • Any notices, petitions or orders already received
  • Income proof for both parties, if available
  • Children’s birth certificates and school records

Typical timeline: mutual consent, 6–18 months. Contested matters vary widely. We give you a realistic range at the first meeting rather than an optimistic one.

What this covers

  • Regular bail and anticipatory bail
  • FIR quashing and Section 528 BNSS petitions
  • Trial representation before Magistrate and Sessions courts
  • Appeals and revisions
  • Complaints, private complaints and counter-cases
  • Economic offences and cheating matters
  • Matters under the POCSO and SC/ST Acts

Bring to the first meeting

  • Copy of the FIR or complaint, if you have it
  • Any notice under Section 35(3) BNSS
  • Names of the police station and investigating officer
  • Identity and address proof

Urgent? Bail applications are time-critical. Call rather than email if an arrest is imminent or has already happened.

02 — Criminal Defence

Criminal Defence

The first forty-eight hours after an FIR shape much of what follows. If you or a family member has been named in one, the useful move is to get advice before anything is said to anyone.

We appear in matters under the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita and the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, from bail through trial and appeal. That includes anticipatory bail applications, quashing petitions where an FIR is plainly an abuse of process, and defence at trial before the Magistrate and Sessions courts in Pune.

An accused person is entitled to a defence. We treat that as the baseline, not a favour.

03 — Civil & Property

Civil & Property

Most property disputes we are asked to fix were avoidable two years earlier, at the due-diligence stage. We do both halves of the work: the litigation, and the paperwork that keeps you out of it.

On the contentious side: partition suits, declaration and injunction, specific performance, tenancy and eviction, easement disputes, and recovery of possession. On the advisory side: title search and verification, sale and gift deeds, development and lease agreements, wills, and society conveyance.

Pune’s eastern corridor has seen enough resale and redevelopment activity that a proper title search before you pay a token amount is money extremely well spent.

Discuss a property matter

What this covers

  • Title search, verification and legal opinion
  • Partition, declaration and injunction suits
  • Specific performance of agreements
  • Tenancy, eviction and rent control matters
  • Sale deeds, gift deeds, wills and agreements
  • Society matters and deemed conveyance
  • Builder delay and possession disputes

Bring to the first meeting

  • 7/12 extract, index II, or the sale deed
  • Property card and mutation entries
  • Any agreement, notice or correspondence
  • Survey number and full property description

Note: a title search is usually a fixed-fee engagement and can be turned around in a week or two.

What this covers

  • MACT compensation claims — injury and fatal
  • Enhancement appeals against inadequate awards
  • Insurance repudiation and delay disputes
  • Hit-and-run and unidentified vehicle claims
  • Defence in rash and negligent driving prosecutions
  • Licence suspension and challan matters

Bring to the first meeting

  • FIR, spot panchnama and charge sheet copy
  • Medical records, bills and disability certificate
  • Salary slips or income proof of the injured/deceased
  • Vehicle RC, insurance policy and driving licence

Important: a claim petition under Section 166 of the Motor Vehicles Act has no limitation period, but evidence gets harder to gather with every month that passes. File early.

04 — Motor Accident & Traffic

Motor Accident & Traffic

After a serious road accident, families are usually dealing with hospitals and grief at exactly the moment the paperwork has to start. We take that part over.

We represent claimants before the Motor Accident Claims Tribunal in Pune for injury and fatal accident compensation, pursue enhancement appeals where a tribunal has undervalued a claim, and contest insurer repudiations. We also defend rash and negligent driving prosecutions and handle licence and challan matters.

Compensation is computed on a settled framework — income, age, dependency, and the nature of the disability. We will show you the working, so you understand what your claim is actually worth.

05 — Cheque Bounce & Recovery

Cheque Bounce & Recovery

A dishonoured cheque has a strict clock attached to it. Miss the notice window and a good claim becomes a difficult one.

We file and defend complaints under Section 138 of the Negotiable Instruments Act, draft and answer statutory demand notices, and pursue commercial recovery through summary suits under Order XXXVII — through to execution, which is the stage most claimants underestimate.

We also advise on the defence side, where a complaint has been filed on a security cheque or an inflated amount.

Discuss a recovery matter

The Section 138 clock

StepDeadline
Present the chequeWithin 3 months of the date on it
Issue demand noticeWithin 30 days of the return memo
Drawer’s time to pay15 days from receipt of notice
File the complaintWithin 30 days after that

Bring to the first meeting

  • The original cheque and the bank return memo
  • Proof of the underlying debt or liability
  • Any notice already issued or received
  • Correspondence with the other party

Timelines above are the general position under the Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881. Get the dates checked against your facts before relying on them.

What this covers

  • District and State Consumer Commission complaints
  • Builder delay, possession and RERA matters
  • Insurance and banking service deficiency
  • Medical negligence claims
  • Wrongful termination and unpaid dues
  • Gratuity, provident fund and settlement disputes
  • POSH complaints and workplace inquiries

Bring to the first meeting

  • The invoice, policy or agreement in question
  • Written complaints already made, and any reply
  • Proof of payment and of the loss suffered
  • Appointment letter and payslips, for employment matters

Limitation: a consumer complaint is generally to be filed within two years of the cause of action.

06 — Consumer & Employment

Consumer & Employment

Two areas where the claim is often small enough that people give up, and the other side is counting on exactly that.

Consumer commissions are designed to be accessible, and a well-drafted complaint with clean documentary proof frequently resolves at the notice stage. We handle builder delay and possession claims, insurance and banking service deficiency, and medical negligence matters.

On the employment side we act for employees in termination, unpaid dues, gratuity and provident fund disputes, and advise on POSH complaints and internal inquiries.
